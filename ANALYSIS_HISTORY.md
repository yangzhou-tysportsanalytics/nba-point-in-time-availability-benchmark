# Analysis history

The table below records, for each analysis in the paper, when its plan was written and which results were already known at that point. It is the basis for the statement in the paper that the retrospective estimates were not obtained blind.

| Corrections after an independent audit of the code (2026-09-26) | Three defects, all in robustness runs, all fixed and rerun. Two share a cause: a player's recent history was looked up by (this game, player) among the players who appear in that game's box score, and an absent player never does, so every absent player was valued at zero in the plus-minus run (55,036 of 55,036 rows) and the ``without the 240-minute rescaling'' run left 90.6% of its rows rescaled. The corrected figures are 0.051 (0.001–0.107)\ and −0.030 (−0.067 to 0.004)\ under the plus-minus valuation and 0.205 (0.093–0.309)\ and 0.038 (−0.042 to 0.122)\ without the rescaling, and both are now reported as valuation-specific rather than as a portability claim. The third: the eligibility rule for the early cutoffs of the paper was applied to the holdout but not to training, and with it applied to both, almost all of the early-cutoff spike is missing reporting rather than late news. The no-fallback six-hour benchmark beside the hybrid rule (the table below), the pooled headline on the public rebuild (the paper) and the narrowing of the mechanism claim to ``reading the report later helps'' were added in the same round. |
| Put-back tests of the decomposition components (the paper) | Exploratory follow-ups, designed after the subgroup contrast of the same section had been run and found not to resolve under the reported design, and after the decomposition identity and the paired cutoff contrasts were known. The never-confirmed components (b) and (c) were put back first, in plan N, when the wording under test was the negative half of the mechanism claim; component (a) was added in the following round, when its null would have contradicted the text we then had. All are retrospective: each component is defined on players who did not play. |
| Rebuild of the decomposition, after an independent reproduction of the runs (2026-09-26) | The decomposition of the paper did not hold as written: its first component was a *net* change in Out value rather than the value newly declared Out, and its other two were not disjoint from the compliant feature's own Out set, so a player already counted by F1—one Out at the cutoff whose status was withdrawn on a later report, and who then sat—was counted twice. The section is rebuilt on the identity now printed there, checked player-game by player-game and game by game. The dose-responses barely move; the sentence ``the thirty-minute report is the six-hour report with the late Out designations added'' was false, and the exclusive attribution that rested on it is withdrawn. Corrected in the same round: the withdrawn plus-minus claim still in the paper; the stale put-back figure in the conclusion; Figure fig:taxonomy left at the superseded twelve-hour run and mixing six different contrasts under one heading; a sentence in the paper that gave the hyperparameter choice a neutrality it cannot have; two references cited as though they guaranteed the bootstrap's coverage; the fallback counts quoted as a rate that includes the team-games it fails to serve; and a ``both intervals reach zero'' that was true of one of them. The plus-minus valuation was rerun at 10,000 replicates under five seeds, and its lower bound straddles zero, which the text now reports. The shared history lookup was found not to carry the main build's 365-day window, so the no-rescale run was an approximate ablation—with the window restored it reproduces the build's reference minutes for all but 10 of 178,421 rows—the exception, found in the next round, is the build's cap on reference minutes, largest difference 1.06 minutes—and the estimates move by 0.001–0.002. |
| Calibration of the bootstrap designs (2026-10-04) | Planned and run after a review argued that the two-way team bootstrap we had reported was miscalibrated. All headline results were known, under that design. The calibration is a placebo study on the real schedule with a known truth, so its reading rule needed no new judgement: a design either covers at its nominal rate or it does not. It confirmed the review on the mechanics -- the two-way weights give a variance of 2.66 against 1.00 and coverage near 1.000 -- and contradicted its diagnosis, because the per-game loss difference carries almost no team structure (0.3%) and what keeps the higher designs wide is the season. That is why the paper now reports both targets rather than simply narrowing the interval. |
| Single-season power, and the amendment to the registration (2026-10-05) | Computed before the design for the prospective test was chosen, and deliberately in that order: the question was which design the 2026--27 test should use, and a design cannot be chosen on a calibration alone when its cost is power. All retrospective results were known; no 2026--27 game had been played. The amendment was filed on OSF on 2026-10-05, fifteen days before the season opened. |
| Lagged-participation baseline (2026-10-05) | Added after a review asked the obvious harder question: a team's previous box score is public and free, so the report must beat it, not only beat a model with no availability information. All headline results were known. The contrast could have gone against the paper -- if the report had added nothing over last night's absences, the headline would have been about absence mattering rather than about the report carrying information -- and it did not. |
| The deployment gap, pooled (2026-10-05) | Added in the same round, after a review pointed out that the paper calls this the number a buyer should ask for and then reports it only as a range across a grid on one season. All results were known, including the 2025--26 value. Pooling moved it down, from 0.185 on that season to 0.068 over the four holdouts, and the generalising interval reaches zero; both readings are now printed. |
